    How the needles work

    Each needle shows one estimate: the chance the candidate on that side wins. It is this site's own rough model, not a professional forecast.

    • Before votes are counted the needle sits on its starting point (below), allowing about 5 to 6 points of polling error.
    • As counties report, each county's count is compared with how it voted for president in 2024. The average shift in counties that are mostly counted is applied to the votes still to come everywhere. Partly counted counties count for less, because early and mail ballots, which often lean one way, are usually counted first.
    • Results elsewhere count too. Polling misses tend to run the same way across the country, so part of any state's surprise is treated as shared. When a state's official count runs ahead of or behind its starting point (say, Democrats doing much worse than expected in Florida), every other race's starting point moves by the shared part. The move is bigger the more of that state's vote is in, and each card says how far it moved. A state's own count still outweighs this as its votes come in. Only official counts feed this, never market prices.
    • Early clues. Five House races that report early and fast (Florida's 14th and 25th, Virginia's 1st, 2nd and 5th, from Axios's list) work the same way. Each has an expected result: its 2024 presidential margin, plus the forecast national swing, plus about 2 points for an incumbent. When a district's count runs ahead of or behind that, the shared part moves every Senate needle. Districts count for less than a statewide race, and only once a few precincts are in.
    • The range under each needle is the middle 80% of likely final margins. It narrows as the share of the expected vote still out shrinks.
    • Market ticks (K for Kalshi, P for Polymarket) are betting prices, shown separately. They are not part of the estimate. The hollow mark is the starting point before the count.
    • Race calls appear only when a news organization's own post says so, with a link. The needle keeps showing the model.
    • G. Elliott Morris's analysis is built in. Each needle's starting point blends the final polling average (30%) with his final forecast (70%). When he posts a win probability for a race on Bluesky tonight, it is blended into that needle at up to half weight, fading by half each hour and as the official count comes in.
    • Where the counts come from. Ohio, Florida, Alaska and Nebraska are read straight from their official election-night files: county by county, except Nebraska, which gives statewide totals only. Texas, Michigan, South Carolina, Iowa and Kansas block automated readers, and Maine posts no live count. Their needles show the Kalshi and Polymarket average, labelled "market odds". A counted state whose feed stops answering after its polls close switches to market odds the same way.
    • Alaska counts absentee ballots for two weeks and uses ranked choice, so its range stays wide.
