    How the needles work

    Each needle shows one estimate: the chance the candidate on that side wins. It is this site's own rough model, not a professional forecast.

    • Before votes are counted the needle sits on the final polling average for that race, allowing about 5 to 6 points of polling error.
    • As counties report, each county's count is compared with how it voted for president in 2024. The average shift in counties that are mostly counted is applied to the votes still to come everywhere. Partly counted counties count for less, because early and mail ballots, which often lean one way, are usually counted first.
    • The range under each needle is the middle 80% of likely final margins. It narrows as the share of the expected vote still out shrinks.
    • Market ticks (K for Kalshi, P for Polymarket) are betting prices, shown separately. They are not part of the estimate. The hollow tick is the pre-election polling average.
    • Race calls appear only when a news organization's own post says so, with a link. The needle keeps showing the model.
    • G. Elliott Morris's analysis (Strength In Numbers, FiftyPlusOne) is pinned at the top of the feed from his public Bluesky account, and his latest post on each race appears on its card. His posts are his own words, linked to the original.
    • Where the counts come from. Ohio, Florida, Alaska and Nebraska are read straight from their official election-night files: county by county, except Nebraska, which gives statewide totals only. Texas, Michigan, South Carolina, Iowa and Kansas block automated readers, and Maine posts no live count. Their needles show the Kalshi and Polymarket average, labelled "market odds". A counted state whose feed stops answering after its polls close switches to market odds the same way.
    • Alaska counts absentee ballots for two weeks and uses ranked choice, so its range stays wide.

    Posts and headlines are shown as short excerpts with links to the original. Sources: state election offices, Kalshi, Polymarket, Bluesky, Google News; 2024 county results compiled by T. McGovern from official returns. Source code.
